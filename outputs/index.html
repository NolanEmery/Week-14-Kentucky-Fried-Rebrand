1952 / Corbin, Kentucky

The Roadside Kitchen

It started with a pressure cooker, a small motel kitchen, and a recipe that made strangers pull over. Colonel Sanders served chicken one plate at a time—because good food was worth stopping for.

SKIP TO THE BOOM
1960s—70s / Everywhere

The Franchise Boom

The white suit went on the road. One hand-painted sign became hundreds, then thousands. The Colonel’s recipe travelled farther than he ever could—and a little piece of Kentucky went with it.

SKIP TO BUCKET CULTURE
1990s—2000s / The shareable years

Bucket Culture

Meals got louder, tables got fuller, and the bucket became more than dinner. It was the middle of a game night, a family car ride, a Friday that needed no further explanation.

SKIP TO THE SLUMP
A hard look / A needed reset

The Slump

The world changed its appetite. We heard the questions—about what we made, how we made it, and what a fast-food favourite should feel like now. So we went back to the kitchen.

SKIP TO NEW KFC
Today / Still unmistakably us

New KFC

Same roots. More ways in. We’re keeping the flavour, opening up the menu, and making room at the table for how people eat today. The next great KFC story is still being written.
